# E2: causal vs passenger methylation under stabilising selection

Code: `scripts/slim/e2_stab.slim`, `scripts/python/{model,e2,e2_plot}.py`. Results: `csv/e2/`,
`pdf/e2/e2_causal_passenger.pdf`. Model: `pdf/theory.pdf` §3–7, §11. Run 2026-10-01; ~10 min on 9 cores.

## Design

- **Shared setup.** Same occupancy and methylation maps as E1 (a₁ = 0), methylation-gated mutation (r = 20), Q = 2
  (N = 100), burn-in 5/u, measurement 2/u. 30 replicates per condition.
- **P (passenger).** g = O, with an intermediate optimum E* = 0.5. That means 6 of 8 motif positions match, and 28
  motif configurations are optimal.
- **C (causal).** g = exp(−R/R₀), where R = Σ m_i over the 300-bp locus and R₀ = 3. Methylated CpGs repress, and only
  where there are CpGs.
- **Fitness.** w = exp(−κ(E − E*)²) with κ ∈ {2, 10}. M0 is the neutral reference.
- **Matching (two stages).** P runs first and C's optimum is set from P's equilibrium.
  - **C_k2 / C_k10:** E* = the mean of exp(−R/R₀) over P haplotypes. This matched C_k2 on R (2.56 vs 2.55) but not
    C_k10 (1.91 vs 2.56). Under strong selection C narrows the R distribution, and Jensen's inequality then moves its
    mean.
  - **C_k10_Rmatch:** E* = exp(−R̄_P/R₀). This gives R = 2.72 vs 2.56, matched to within 0.1 of the between-haplotype
    SD (1.5).
- **Validation.** SLiM and Python agree on mean expression to ≤ 5×10⁻⁷ across 600 snapshots.

## Results

| | M0 | P κ2 | P κ10 | C κ2 | C κ10 | C κ10 R-matched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean methylation load R | 2.60 | 2.55 | 2.56 | 2.56 | 1.91 | 2.72 |
| SD of R over time (consensus) | 1.48 | 1.44 | 1.43 | 0.48 | **0.07** | **0.30** |
| \|ΔR\| over 2/u generations | 1.87 | 1.74 | 1.64 | 0.52 | **0.04** | **0.22** |
| CpG positional turnover over 2/u | 0.99 | 0.99 | 0.98 | 0.99 | 0.99 | 1.00 |
| TF occupancy O | 0.01 | **0.50** | **0.50** | 0.00 | 0.01 | 0.06 |
| motif positional turnover over 2/u | 0.37 | **0.00** | **0.01** | 0.39 | 0.32 | 0.33 |
| MAF, variants changing R only | 0.126 | 0.121 | 0.126 | 0.107 | **0.065** | **0.095** |
| MAF, variants changing neither | 0.124 | 0.125 | 0.121 | 0.122 | 0.111 | 0.120 |
| realised 2Ns_het, R-only variants | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.6 | 11.4 | 8.1 |
| P/D, loss of methylated CpGs (÷ M0) | 1.00 ± 0.06 | 0.98 ± 0.05 | 1.03 ± 0.05 | 1.21 ± 0.07 | **2.58 ± 0.26** | **1.89 ± 0.12** |

1. **Methylation can be conserved while every CpG position turns over (CHATGPT Q3: yes).** Under C, κ = 10, the
   locus's methylation load barely moves over 2/u generations (|ΔR| = 0.04–0.22, vs 1.6–1.9 under P/M0). Meanwhile
   98–100% of CpG positions are replaced. Gains compensate losses anywhere in the locus. This is the degenerate-map
   prediction, and it is invisible to per-position conservation scores.
2. **Degeneracy alone does not produce turnover.** P has 28 optimal motif configurations, yet its motif positions
   almost never turn over (0–0.01). Moving between two 6-of-8 configurations means passing through 5 or 7 matches,
   which costs 2Ns ≈ 14–72. In C, each step changes R by about one CpG, with per-step 2Ns ≈ 2–11, so compensation is
   possible. **Turnover needs a degenerate map *and* individually weak steps.**
3. **Passenger methylation is invisible to allele-frequency and P/D tests.** Under P, variants that change methylation
   load but not occupancy have exactly neutral MAF (0.121–0.126 vs 0.126) and neutral P/D (0.98–1.03). This holds even
   though P maintains a TF that sets local methylation. Under C both statistics shift: MAF −24% to −48%, and P/D
   1.9–2.6 at 2Ns ≈ 8–11. At κ = 2 (2Ns ≈ 1.6) the shift is marginal: MAF −15%, P/D 1.21 ± 0.07.
4. **In C the TF is lost.** Fitness reads methylation, and TF protection affects too few CpGs to be worth maintaining
   (motif 2Ns_het ≈ 0.3–2.4). So the cleanest field signature of C vs P in this toy is the motif: P conserves it and C
   lets it decay. A real causal element would most likely need both, which is the next model (C + P, below).
5. **Stabilising P/D is smaller than P0's directional prediction.** P0 gives P/D ≈ 2 at S = 1.6, but C κ = 2 shows
   1.21. Near an optimum, selection on a small effect is second order (s ≈ κ(Δg/2)²), and a loss can be compensated by
   a later gain. P0's directional numbers are an upper bound for stabilising selection on degenerate traits.

## Caveats

- θ is ~20× human, so recurrent CpG mutation is inflated. Polymorphism statistics should be re-run at realistic θ
  before comparing with data (`md/REAL_DATA.md` §1).
- Some statistics come from few variants. The O-class MAF (few segregating motif variants, wide SEs) and the
  `2Ns_het` values are second-order, evaluated at the optimum. The first-order term from the population mean sitting
  off-optimum is not included.
- R is summed over the whole 300-bp locus, which is a strong degeneracy choice. A per-CpG readout would behave like P.

## Next model worth running

**C + P (methylation and TF both causal):** g = O·exp(−R/R₀), or a methylation-sensitive TF where m reduces O
(Domcke 2015). This is the realistic case and the hard one for the statistics above.
