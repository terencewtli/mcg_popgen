# E4: does the causal-methylation signal survive meQTL detection?

Code: `scripts/python/e4.py`. Results: `csv/e4/detection.csv`, `pdf/e4/e4_detection.pdf`. Run 2026-10-01 (44 s; uses
the realistic-θ variants from E2b and E3, no new simulation).

## Design

**Input.** Each segregating-variant snapshot from E2b/E3 has a true derived-allele frequency p and a per-allele effect
β = ΔR on regional methylation load.

**Detection model:**
- **Phenotype:** individual methylation load (sum over the two haplotypes) plus N(0, σ²) noise.
- **σ = 3,** chosen so that a methylated CpG-loss variant (|β| ≈ 0.95) at MAF 0.1 explains ~2% of variance, a typical
  strong cis-meQTL.
- **Detection probability:** P(χ²₁(λ) > c), with λ = n·2p(1−p)β²/σ², at p < 10⁻⁶ and MAF ≥ 0.01, for
  n = 500 / 5,000 / 30,000 (WGBS-panel / HPRC-to-cohort / GoDMC scale).
- **Weighting:** detected sets are power-weighted (expected values).

**Classes:**
- **CpG-SNP meQTL:** changes R, not occupancy.
- **Motif-SNP meQTL:** changes occupancy, and R through protection.

**The test:** the ascertainment-matched MAF ratio. This is the mean MAF of detected meQTLs divided by the same
quantity in the neutral model, put through identical detection.

## Results

**Detected CpG-SNP meQTLs, mean MAF ÷ neutral (same detection):**

| model | n = 500 | 5,000 | 30,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| passenger P / P (CRE) / meth-sensitive TF | 1.00 / 1.00 / 1.01 | 1.02 / 1.02 / 1.05 | 0.99 / 1.01 / 1.02 |
| C κ=2 (2Ns ≈ 1.6) | 1.00 | 0.94 | 0.90 |
| C κ=10 R-matched (2Ns ≈ 8) | 0.93 | 0.98 | 0.96 |
| C κ=10 (2Ns ≈ 11) | 0.75 | 0.57 | **0.50** |
| TF + meth κ=10 (2Ns ≈ 2) | 0.95 | 0.82 | 0.73 |
| TF + meth κ=40 (2Ns ≈ 8) | 0.85 | 0.56 | **0.45** |

(SE ≈ 0.01–0.03, except TF + meth κ=40 at n = 500: 0.07.)

1. **The ascertainment-matched test has the right null.** All three passenger models sit at 1.00 ± 0.05 at every n.
2. **It detects causal methylation when per-CpG selection is strong enough, and power grows with n.** C κ=10 and
   TF + meth κ=40 drop to 0.45–0.50 at n = 30,000; TF + meth κ=10 to 0.73; C κ=2 to 0.90.
3. **It is weaker than the full-population statistics in E2b/E3.** C κ=10 R-matched has P/D 8.3 and DAF ratio 0.69,
   but its detected-meQTL MAF ratio is only 0.96–0.98. Selection acts mostly on the *rarest* variants. MAF ≥ 0.01
   plus a power weighting that favours common variants removes them. **Signal lives below the usual MAF filter.**
4. **The naive effect–frequency correlation is biased under the null.** Among detected CpG-SNP meQTLs, corr(|β|, MAF)
   is −0.09 to −0.13 under passenger models (n ≥ 5,000), with no selection on any CpG. That is as negative as causal
   C κ=10 (−0.05 to −0.08). The cause is detection bias:
   - CpGs near a bound TF are partly protected, so effect sizes vary;
   - large-effect variants are detected at lower MAF.
   (In the neutral model the correlation is ≈ 0 only because the motif has decayed and all effects are ≈ 0.95.)
   **Pooled effect–MAF correlations, as reported descriptively for meQTLs, are not evidence of selection without an
   ascertainment-matched null.**
5. **Motif-SNP meQTLs are depleted (MAF ratio 0.04–0.17) whenever a TF is selected.** That covers P, P (CRE),
   meth-sensitive and TF + meth. The motif class therefore shows TF selection, not causal methylation. It confirms the
   mechanism-class logic: **CpG-SNP depletion separates causal from passenger; motif-SNP depletion does not.**
6. **Secondary: effect-sign asymmetry.** Among detected CpG-SNP meQTLs, the fraction whose derived allele increases
   methylation is 0.49–0.50 under neutral and passenger, but 0.88–0.96 (C κ=10) or 0.03–0.17 (C κ=10 R-matched)
   under causal. The direction depends on which side of the optimum the population sits, so it is model-specific and
   not a general test.

## Caveats

- **Frequency resolution.** N = 100 gives a minimum MAF of 0.005, so the rare tail where selection acts is barely
  represented. Real samples (n = 30,000, Ne ~ 10⁴) resolve MAF ~ 10⁻³–10⁻⁴. Power for causal methylation is
  therefore probably *underestimated*. The real-N check (`md/ENDPOINT.md` item 8) is the way to quantify this, and is
  the most useful first cluster job.
- **Effect sizes are nearly two-valued** (±0.95 for a methylated CpG gained or lost), so within-class effect–frequency
  *slopes* (BayesS-type S) are not estimable here. Only MAF-based comparisons are.
- **No LD.** Each variant is its own causal meQTL. Real lead SNPs tag causal variants imperfectly, which dilutes every
  statistic. CpG-SNPs are the class least affected, since they are their own mechanism.
- **Regional phenotype.** R is a locus-level load, not single-CpG array methylation. A CpG-SNP that removes the
  measured CpG itself (a probe artefact on arrays) is not modelled.

## Prediction for real data

Test **CpG-SNP meQTLs (outside motifs) against a neutral expectation built with the same detection model**: the
neutral SFS with CpG recurrence, the study's n, σ and threshold. Include low-frequency variants. Do not rely on pooled
effect–MAF correlations. Under passenger methylation the matched ratio is 1. Under causal methylation with per-CpG
2Ns ≳ 2 it is < 1 and falls with n.
