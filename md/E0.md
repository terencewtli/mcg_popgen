# E0: validating the SLiM machinery before adding methylation

Code: `scripts/slim/e0_neutral.slim`, `scripts/slim/e0_fixation.slim`, `scripts/python/oracle.py`,
`scripts/python/e0.py`. Results: `csv/e0/e0_checks.csv`, `pdf/e0/e0_validation.pdf`.

## Conventions (shared by every later experiment)

- SLiM Wright–Fisher, N diploids, 2N haplosomes, Ne = N. One tick is one generation.
- Rates are per site, per haplosome, per generation. Jukes–Cantor background: each of the 3 possible changes at a site
  has rate α, so the total is u = 3α per site.
- CpG transitions (C→T at a CpG C; G→A at a CpG G) have rate r·α, set through SLiM's 64×4 trinucleotide matrix.
  Bases off either chromosome end count as "A" for context (SLiM convention, `chromosome.cpp`). The oracle copies this.
- Gating: a `mutation()` callback accepts each proposed CpG transition with probability g. The effective rate is g·r·α.
  In the methylation models, g becomes the haplotype's methylation-dependent acceptance (1 + (r−1)m)/r. SLiM passes the
  **parental** haplosome to the callback, so gating is exact only when the site is not recombining. The methylation
  models therefore use no recombination.
- Selection: genotype fitnesses are 1, 1 + hs, 1 + s. New mutations are seeded in `late()` so they affect fitness from
  the first offspring generation onward. (Seeding in `early()` makes them neutral for one generation; SLiM warns about it.)

## Expectations and where they come from

**Neutral π (finite sites, JC).** Two lineages coalesce at T ~ Exp(rate 1/2N). Under JC, the probability that the two
sequences match at one site after total branch length 2T is ¼ + ¾·exp(−(4u/3)·2T). Averaging over T gives

E[identity] = ¼ + ¾ / (1 + 16Nu/3), so **π = θ / (1 + 4θ/3), with θ = 4Nu**.

With θ = 0.01 this is π = 0.009868 (infinite sites would give 0.01).

**Folded SFS.** Under the standard neutral coalescent, ξᵢ ∝ 1/i (infinite sites, θ small). Folding gives ∝ 1/i + 1/(n−i),
with i = n/2 counted once. θ = 0.01 per site makes multiple hits rare enough to ignore. Recombination (4Nr = 0.4 per bp)
only shrinks the variance between loci; it leaves the expectation unchanged.

**Fixation (Kimura).** With fitnesses 1, 1 + s/2, 1 + s, the drift mean is M(x) = (s/2)·x(1−x) and the variance is
V(x) = x(1−x)/2N. Then 2M/V = 2Ns, and u(p) = (1 − e^(−2Ns·p)) / (1 − e^(−2Ns)). At p = 1/2N:

**u = (1 − e^(−s)) / (1 − e^(−2Ns))**, which is 1/2N at s = 0. The scale to report is **2Ns**, not s.

**CpG trajectory (oracle).** Under neutrality, a sampled haplosome at generation t is the ancestral sequence carried
along one lineage for t generations of mutation. Drift only changes which lineage gets sampled. So E[CpG count](t) in
SLiM must equal that of the single-sequence Markov chain started from the same ancestral sequence. The oracle simulates
that chain exactly (Gillespie, same 64×4 matrix and edge rule). No equilibrium is needed, so the test is sharp even
though the composition equilibrates on a 1/u timescale, far longer than N.

**Rescaling (Q).** Using N/Q, u·Q, r_rec·Q and generations/Q keeps θ, ρ and the elapsed time in units of N fixed. π and the
SFS should not change with Q until per-generation rates stop being small.

## Status

**2026-10-01: all 18 checks pass (|z| < 3; largest |z| = 2.29).** The full run takes 44 s wall-clock on 9 cores; raw
output is 7.4 MB in `sim/e0/` (not mirrored).

|---|---|---|

| fixation, 20,000 trials | 2Ns = −2, 0, 1, 2, 5, 10 (N=100); 0, 5 (N=50) | all within 1.3 SE of Kimura |

What this licenses: the trinucleotide matrix, the edge convention, `mutation()`-callback rejection, `fitnessEffect`-free
selection and Q-rescaling of neutral statistics all behave as derived. Not yet tested: Q-invariance **with selection on a
sequence-encoded trait** (this is E1's first check), and anything involving `fitnessEffect()`.

A free sanity number: equilibrium CpG content in this crude JC model is 9.4 CpG/kb at r = 20 (o/e ≈ 0.15 against the
62.4/kb expected at uniform composition) and 17.4/kb at r = 10 (o/e ≈ 0.28). Bulk human CpG o/e is ~0.2–0.25, so
r ≈ 10–20 is in the right range. This is the Sved & Bird (1990) style equilibrium, recovered by forward simulation.
