# E3: TF and methylation both matter

Code: `scripts/slim/e3_combined.slim`, `scripts/python/{model,e3,e3_plot}.py`. Results: `csv/e3/`,
`pdf/e3/e3_combined.pdf`. Run 2026-10-01: 200 runs, about 22 min on 9 cores. SLiM and Python agree on the readout to
≤ 5×10⁻⁷.

## Design

All conditions are at realistic θ (as in E2b) with methylation-gated mutation (r = 20) and N = 100. Each run starts
from a random sequence carrying a perfect motif. It burns in for 5/u at high θ (α = 2e-5) so composition reaches
equilibrium, then switches to θ = 0.001 (`setMutationMatrix`) for 20N + 0.5/u generations. 40 replicates.

| condition | readout g | motif | optimum E* |
|---|---|---|---|
| M0 | neutral | TGACTCAT | none |
| **CP κ=10 / κ=40** | O·exp(−R/R₀) | TGACTCAT | 0.5·exp(−R̄_P/R₀) = 0.219: the E2 passenger equilibrium is one optimal point among many O–R trade-offs |
| **Pcre** (control) | O | CRE TGACGTCA, CpG required for binding | O(7 matches) = 0.881 |
| **MS** (methylation-sensitive TF) | O·(1 − m_c), m_c = methylation of the motif CpG | same | O₇(1 − m_c,7) = 0.799 |

Two modelling choices came out of the smoke tests:
1. **The CRE CpG is required for binding** (O = 0 without it), as for CREB. Without this, MS simply mutates its CpG
   away, because all motif positions otherwise count equally. That is an artefact.
2. **The CRE optima sit at 7 matches.** At 6 matches MS's discrete readout (0.25 / 0.80 / 0.93 for 6 / 7 / 8 matches)
   makes the 6/7 heterozygote fittest. That overdominance would keep the motif polymorphic and contaminate the
   frequency statistics.

Not modelled: the bound-vs-methylated feedback loop. Protection of m_c uses raw O, not O·(1 − m_c), so there is no
bistability.

## Results

| | M0 | Pcre | MS | CP κ10 | CP κ40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| occupancy O | 0.02 | 0.88 | 0.88 | 0.51 | 0.78 |
| methylation load R | 2.76 | 2.57 | 2.72 | 2.64 | 3.72 |
| SD of R over time | 1.30 | 1.12 | 1.20 | **0.15** | **0.06** |
| motif CpG present | | 1.00 | 1.00 | | |
| P/D, loss of methylated CpGs outside motif (÷ M0) | 1.00 ± 0.05 | 1.05 ± 0.05 | 1.09 ± 0.06 | **3.5 ± 0.3** | **14 ± 3** |
| DAF ÷ neutral: R-only variants | 1 | 0.98 | 1.00 | **0.56** | **0.27** |
| DAF ÷ neutral: motif (non-CpG) | 1 | 0.05 | 0.03 | 0.08 | 0.08 |
| DAF ÷ neutral: motif CpG | | 0.024 | 0.024 | | |
| realised 2Ns_het: R-only / motif / motif CpG | | 0 / 60 / 390 | 0 / 130 / 320 | 2.1 / 15 / – | 8.3 / 35 / – |

1. **The combined model shows both signatures.** CP conserves the motif (6–7 matches, no motif turnover, motif DAF 8%
   of neutral) like P, and conserves R with selected CpG-only variants (P/D 3.5–14, DAF 27–56% of neutral) like C.
   - So the passenger-vs-causal test from E2/E2b is really **"is there selection on CpG-only variants?"** That
     question is answerable whether or not a TF is also under selection.
   - **A conserved motif does not imply passenger methylation.**
2. **Alternative optima: the same expression can be reached by different TF/methylation combinations, and lineages
   commit to one.** Replicates are independent lineages (species, in effect), and CP settles into one of two states:
   - **6 matches with R ≈ 2.5:** 39/40 runs at κ=10, 11/40 at κ=40;
   - **7 matches with R ≈ 4.2:** 1/40 at κ=10, 29/40 at κ=40.
   - Stronger binding is offset by more methylation.
   - No run switches state during 0.5/u at realistic θ. The state is chosen during the burn-in, and switching would
     cross a valley, as in E2b.
   - **Implication for comparative data:** orthologous elements with matched expression can differ in *both* motif
     strength and methylation across species without any change in selection. Methylation divergence is then not
     evidence of expression divergence. This is a sequence-level analogue of developmental systems drift.
3. **The reverse arrow (methylation blocks binding) is invisible here.** MS and its control Pcre are
   indistinguishable on every statistic:
   - CpG-only variants are neutral in both (P/D ≈ 1, DAF ≈ 1);
   - the motif CpG is under strong selection in both (DAF 2.4% of neutral, 2Ns ≈ 300–400);
   - the motif CpG is always present in both.
   The reason is **self-protection.** A strongly bound TF keeps its own CpG unmethylated (m_c ≈ 0.09), so the
   sensitivity rarely bites, and the CpG is selected anyway because binding requires it. Population-genetic data from
   the bound state cannot detect methylation sensitivity. It would show up only where the TF is absent or weak, e.g.
   tissues or lineages without it, or in the feedback model that is not run here.

## What it adds to the endpoint predictions

- The causal-methylation test is selection on **CpG-only variants outside the motif**. It holds alone (C) and with a
  selected TF (CP), and it is absent for passenger and methylation-sensitive TFs.
- Motif conservation and methylation-sensitivity are not diagnostic of causal methylation.
- Divergence in TF strength plus methylation at constant expression is expected under CP. Comparative analyses should
  not read methylation divergence alone as functional divergence.
