# Review of the simulation phase (cluster session, 2026-10-02)

Scope: the math in `tex/theory.tex` / `pdf/theory.pdf`, the claims in `md/SUMMARY.md` (F1–F10, R1–R7), and the
cluster hand-off in `md/ENDPOINT.md` / `md/REAL_DATA.md`. Checks were done by re-deriving formulas and recomputing
numbers independently, not by re-running SLiM. The design plan that uses these results is
`realdata/md/DESIGN_PLAN.md`.

## Verdict in one paragraph

The core derivations are correct and the simulator validation is careful:
- fixation probability, finite-sites diversity, the E1 two-state equilibrium, the origin-fixation stationary
  distribution and the Sawyer–Hartl P/D numbers all re-derive or recompute exactly.

The most important qualitative results are well supported:
- **passenger methylation is invisible to frequency statistics (F4);**
- **CpG-loss P/D separates a mutation-rate change from selection (F3);**
- **naive effect–MAF correlations are biased by detection (F7).**

Two things need correcting or qualifying before the real-data phase leans on them.
1. **F2 overstates the ridge.** CpG o/e and cross-species conservation are *each* degenerate in (germline m, S),
   but along *different* ridges. Jointly they identify (m, S) in the model, given the gain rate γ and the
   hypermutability r. This is a correction, and it adds a second test.
2. **P/D magnitudes are not interpretable as S under stabilising selection.** E2b already shows this (8 vs 158);
   only direction and ordering transfer to real data.

The proposed first cluster job (the real-N SLiM check) is lower value than stated and needs re-scoping.

## 2. Correction to F2 / PRIORS "the same ridge"

- **The claim.** A CGI-like o/e of 0.6 arises either from germline m ≈ 0.15 with S = 0 or from m = 1 with S ≈ 1.2
  (true). PRIORS then says that "divergence or composition data alone cannot separate them" and that conservation
  shows "the same ridge".
- **What the model actually says.**
  - Along the o/e ridge f is fixed. Solving f = b/(a+b) for ℓ gives a total turnover rate of
    **a + b = α·γ·φ(S)/f**, which *increases* with S.
  - So at matched o/e, the selection route predicts **lower** orthologous CpG conservation: high loss is balanced by
    selected gains, so there is more flux.
  - Recomputed from the formulas (o/e fixed at 0.6):

| r | route A: m, S = 0 | route B: m = 1, S | human–macaque P(conserved), A vs B | turnover B / A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.237 | 0.85 | 0.80 vs 0.72 | 1.48 |
| 15 | 0.152 | 1.20 | 0.80 vs 0.69 | 1.72 |
| 20 | 0.112 | 1.46 | 0.80 vs 0.66 | 1.90 |

- **So o/e and conservation each lie on a ridge, but different ridges.** Jointly they identify (m, S). PRIORS
  panel C compares an unmethylated neutral CpG with a methylated S ≈ 2 CpG; those differ in o/e, so it is not a test
  of the matched-o/e question.
- **Why P/D remains the better primary test.** The joint (o/e, conservation) identification needs γ (gain rate,
  context-dependent), r and α to be known. P/D cancels all of them. Use the joint test as a **secondary, independent**
  check (DESIGN_PLAN R3).
- **A counter-intuitive prediction worth stating:** in this model, *selection-maintained CpGs are less conserved
  than mutation-maintained CpGs at the same CpG density.* Conservation is not a monotone proxy for constraint on CpGs.

## 3. Logic checks on the findings and predictions

| item | assessment |
|---|---|
| **F1** passive retention 2.6× (3.6× within 10 bp) | Sound mechanism. The magnitude is map-dependent (λ, r, germline protection width), as stated. Real germline LMR widths and r ≈ 10–15 move it both ways; R1's *qualitative* prediction (germline, not soma, predicts excess) is the transferable part. |
| **F3** P/D = 1 under a mutation-rate change | Correct **if germline methylation (hence the rate) was constant over the divergence time.** A lineage-specific change in germline methylation makes P/D ≠ 1 with no selection (rate now ≠ average rate along the branch). Human vs chimp sperm methylomes differ at a minority of regions (Molaro 2011). **Restrict to CpGs with conserved germline methylation in human and chimp**, or model the change. This is the main confounder not in the docs. |
| **F3** magnitudes (P/D 2.1, 8–11) | Under stabilising selection with compensation, P/D sits far below the directional prediction (8 vs 158, E2b). **Do not convert real P/D into S with the P0 formula.** Report direction and ordering across strata, and fit S only with a stabilising model. |
| **F4** passenger invisible | Most robust result; holds across θ, κ and detection. It is also the result the real data can falsify most cleanly (any CpG-SNP selection signal outside motifs rejects pure passenger). |
| **F5** causal depletes CpG-only variants | Supported; the signal is in the rare tail (E4 point 3). In real data this favours **P/D on all CpG-loss variants** over detected-meQTL MAF. |
| **F6** motif-SNP depletion marks the TF | Correct and important: exclude motif-overlapping CpGs from the causal test, or analyse them separately. |
| **F7** effect–MAF bias | Correct. Also note the fix: do not condition on detection at all (see the design plan: effects of CpG-loss alleles are known *a priori*). |
| **F8** turnover only at weak per-CpG S | Supported by the θ comparison. The mechanism (tunnelling) is inferred, not measured; fine as stated. |
| **F10** methylation-sensitive TF undetectable | **Model-specific.** It follows from deterministic self-protection (a bound TF keeps its own CpG unmethylated). Real methyl-sensitive TFs (NRF1, CTCF) have occupancy-dependent, cell-type-variable states. State F10 as "undetectable in this map", not as a general prediction. |
| **E4** ascertainment-matched MAF test | Right null in simulation. In real data the "neutral model put through identical detection" needs the neutral SFS with recurrence and demography *and* the null distribution of β, which is hard to build without circularity. Lower priority than P/D-based designs; keep it as a check. |
| **R7 / analysis D** (mutation-aware null for the Ma et al. sign test) | Our mcg_evo reproduction found that the deposited sign-test results (S7) cannot be reproduced and that the S2 promoter table is internally inconsistent (mcg_evo `docs/REPRODUCTION.md` R04b, R05). **Deprioritise D** until the authors respond; building a null for a result that does not reproduce is premature. |

## 4. The proposed first cluster job (real-N SLiM check)

**What was proposed:** C κ=10 and P κ=10 at N = 10⁴ with human-scale rates, no rescaling, to resolve the rare
frequency tail (minimum MAF 0.005 at N = 100).

**Problems:**
1. **Runtime.** At N = 10⁴ and θ = 0.001, u ≈ 2.5×10⁻⁸ per site, so the E2b measurement window 0.5/u is ~2×10⁷
   generations. That is impractical for SLiM at N = 10⁴ even for a 300-bp locus. Only polymorphism snapshots
   (≈ 20–40N generations after starting from an equilibrium composition) are feasible. P/D's divergence term cannot
   come from it.
2. **Much of the answer is analytic.** For the weak-selection case (2Ns ≈ 1.6, where E2b already matches P0's
   origin-fixation prediction), the rare tail at any N follows from the validated Sawyer–Hartl density. E4's power
   can be integrated over it directly. SLiM is needed only where PRF fails: strong-step stabilising selection with
   compensation (C κ=10).
3. **Diffusion rescaling is exact for the reported quantities** (E0, E1 checks). The real-N run tests discreteness at
   small N, not the regime.

**Recommendation:**
- Re-scope it to *polymorphism-only* snapshots for C κ=10 vs M0 at N = 10⁴, started from E2b's equilibrium
  consensus, as a SLiM job array.
- Do the weak-selection tail analytically.
- It is not on the critical path for the real-data analyses; run it in the background.

## 5. Minor

- o/e is computed against uniform composition (acknowledged). For CGI comparisons use a GC-matched expectation,
  otherwise S estimates absorb base composition.
- Sites are treated independently in P0. The CpG gain rate γ depends strongly on neighbours (TpG/CpA decay products
  are one step from CpG), so γ should be estimated empirically per context class from the background before R3.
- SUMMARY F4's "most robust result" is also the hardest to *use*: a null result in real data (P/D = 1 everywhere)
  cannot distinguish "passenger" from "causal but with per-CpG 2Ns ≲ 1". The design plan addresses this with a
  positive control (CpGs whose selection is not in doubt).
